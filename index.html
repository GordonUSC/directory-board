Four things I need, and then this moves

Everything else on this board I can carry. These four I can't do alone, and Monday is the right room for all of them.

  1. Argue with the scope columns. Now is free. October is expensive.
  2. Give me a real kickoff date. Monday 10 August is a pencil mark I made without asking any of you, so move it freely.
  3. Help me name the data steward, or tell me it's an unbudgeted hire and I'll take that to Joe. An Extended Scholar in this seat would be resume-grade work for one of Alex's recent grads, which solves two things at once.
  4. Tell me honestly whether October 9 is producible. It sits ahead of the Bigglesworth letter of intent window on purpose, but a date none of us can hit costs more than a date we move today.

Who this is for, and why it is us building it

Gordon says this better than I do, so I am borrowing his words. GGP brings universal excellence. Our particular lens, LGBTQ+ professionals and students in the games industry, positions us to serve and center underestimated communities, neurodiverse, Latine, BIPOC, disabled, women, LGBTQIA+, AANHPI, and growing. We serve all audiences, and we are not a niche.

That is not decoration on a production plan. It is the whole reason this thing has to be verified rather than approximate. The families who most need this map are the ones least likely to have somebody at the dinner table who already knows the way in, and a student acting on a date we got wrong pays for our mistake in a way we never see.

2026 is GGP's twentieth year. The Scholars program is seven. Take-Two has been with us every year since 2019, the same year Scholars launched, and they are the ones putting the Directory into the world. That is the story the launch should tell, and I would rather we told it with gratitude than with superlatives.

The people this is actually for

Before any of us argue about story points, this is what the machinery is in service of. Four of ours, in their own right.

  • Joseph Kemp, the first GGP Scholar named to Forbes 30 Under 30 in Games. CEO of Games That Matter, and the creator of Disbarred.
  • iAsia Brown, named Outstanding Alumni by the USC Black Alumni Association, nominated for the Women in Tech Global Awards in two categories, and now the founder of her own scholarship endowment for Los Angeles students in game development.
  • Christopher Kim, scholar, then mentor, then faculty. As an NYU mentor he wrote the thank-you letter to GDC that helped win passes for the next cohort.
  • Jonas Ma, on camera at GDC 2026: "GGP made my entry into the games industry so much easier."

Every one of them found a door. The Directory is the argument that finding the door should not have to depend on luck. If a number on this board does not eventually help produce another one of these, it is the wrong number.

Thank you for reading all of this. Genuinely. Bring your amendments Monday and let's plan next steps!
